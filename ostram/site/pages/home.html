OSeMOSYS for Transmission

Plan power systems with the grid inside each country and the links between them

OSTRAM is an open framework for long-term power planning in the Global South. It builds on OSeMOSYS and OSeMOSYS Global, and it adds what national and regional plans most often leave out: the internal grid of each node, with its losses and costs, and the interconnectors between countries, with their costs, direction and limits, in one least-cost optimisation.

The storyline

Internal transmission and interconnectors together

Power crosses a country's own grid before it reaches demand or a border. OSTRAM models the internal grid of every node, with a loss of {{v:south_asia.internal.transmission_loss|pct}} in South Asia, and the interconnectors between nodes in the same energy balance, so the cost of moving power inside a country competes with the cost of trading it.

Inside a node

One framework

The same pipeline, rules and scenario logic serve every region. A region is a setup: change the inputs, keep the method. The rules that turn settings into limits are written down and run the same way each time.

The rules behind a result

Two data routes

South Asia uses a custom-built dataset from national sources. Latin America and the Caribbean is being built from the regional statistics of OLADE. The site shows how far each route has come.

The model at a glance

For each year from {{v:south_asia.first_year}} to {{v:south_asia.last_year}} and each of {{v:south_asia.timeslices}} time slices, the solver chooses what to build and how much to run so that demand is met at the lowest total cost, within the limits that the scenario sets.
