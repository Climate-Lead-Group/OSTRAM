The model as choices and rules

An OSeMOSYS model answers one question: what is the cheapest way to meet electricity demand in every year from {{v:south_asia.first_year}} to {{v:south_asia.last_year}}? It answers by filling in a very large spreadsheet. Some cells are choices the solver makes; every rule ties some of those cells together. Nothing in the model forecasts or simulates: it picks the cheapest set of numbers that keeps every rule.

Two kinds of choice

Build

New capacity

How many gigawatts of each technology to add in each year: a power plant, a storage unit, a piece of internal grid or an interconnector. In OSeMOSYS this is NewCapacity.

Run

Activity

How much each technology produces or carries in each time slice of each year. In OSeMOSYS this is RateOfActivity. Fuel use, emissions and flows on the grid all follow from it.

Everything else you edit is either a cost that the solver weighs or a limit that it must respect.

What you fix and what the solver decides

You set the capacity that already exists (residual capacity), the least that must be built (minimum investment) and the most that may be built (the build cap). Cost decides everything in between. If a floor is above its cap, no plan can keep both rules and the model has no solution.

The ratios on each technology

Each technology converts what goes in to what comes out. Three ratios describe it: fuel in per unit of activity (InputActivityRatio, the inverse of efficiency), output per unit of activity (OutputActivityRatio) and emissions per unit of activity (EmissionActivityRatio). In OSTRAM the internal grid is a technology like any other: its output ratio is {{v:south_asia.internal.efficiency}}, which is how the internal loss enters the model.

Worked example: one node, one year

Take one node of the South Asia model in one year. The sheet below lists every technology of the node as a row. The first column is the build choice for that year; the other columns are the run choices, one per time slice. Each rule on the left ties some of these cells together. Pick a rule to see which.

{{block:choices_sheet}}

Two things the sheet does not show. First, the node is not alone: the energy balance of each node includes flows on its interconnectors, so the sheets of neighbouring nodes are linked. Second, the year is not alone: what is built in one year stays for the life of the technology, so the sheets of later years start from it. The solver fills every sheet of every node and year at once.

Time slices

Rather than every hour of the year, the model works with {{v:south_asia.timeslices}} time slices: {{v:south_asia.seasons}} seasons, each with {{v:south_asia.daily_time_brackets}} parts of the day. Each time slice has a weight, its share of the year (YearSplit), and demand and capacity factors are given per time slice. Fewer time slices solve faster; more catch more of the daily and seasonal pattern.

Common mistakes

SymptomCauseFix
A plant burns far too little fuelEfficiency entered where the input ratio belongsEnter fuel in per unit out: the inverse of efficiency.
Fuel cost has no effectCost placed on the plant instead of the fuel supply technologyPut fuel prices on the supply (MIN) technology.
The model has no solutionA floor above its cap in some yearCheck minimum investment against the build cap year by year.
A value stays flat for every yearA projection mode that keeps only the first yearUse the projection mode the workbook documents for yearly values.
An edit disappearsThe cell is rewritten by a later stageCheck the compiled input files, not the workbook; see the rules.
