Trade-offs and known limitations

OSTRAM simplifies the power system in the ways listed below. For each choice, the table states what it gains and what it loses, so that a reader knows which results to trust for which purpose.

Trade-offs

ChoiceWhat it gainsWhat it loses
Energy balance with corridors, not power flowTrade between countries and regions, solved fast over decadesLoop flows, impedance and voltage limits; a corridor result is a transfer, not a power-flow solution
One node per country, regions for IndiaA model that solves, with results for each country and each region of IndiaCongestion and siting inside a node
{{v:south_asia.timeslices}} time slicesSolve times that allow many scenariosRamps, short peaks and multi-day weather inside a time slice
An internal grid with one loss and one cost for every nodeInternal transmission enters the trade-off at all, with sourced valuesDifferences in distance, voltage and loss between nodes
Build caps from a proportional ruleTransparent, calibrated limits with no hand entryThe rule sees the existing fleet, not the fleet the model builds, so it needs top-ups
One reserve margin for the whole systemSimplicityAdequacy country by country
Base-year lock and pinThe first years match historyNo optimisation in those years
B as a relaxation of AB never costs more than AB keeps A's limit wherever its own was tighter
C targets as generation floorsPolicy targets met as statedLeast-cost freedom: C buys a given shape of system
