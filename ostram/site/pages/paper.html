The paper

The site and the open-source Global South paper are one product: the paper will be published here with its scenarios. This page holds its outline. It shows no results.

  1. Introduction

    Planning for regional power connectivity needs the grid inside each country and the links between countries in one optimisation, not in separate studies.

  2. OSTRAM as a layer on OSeMOSYS

    The standard OSeMOSYS equations, a base dataset, and a rule layer that turns written settings into the limits of each scenario.

  3. Internal transmission and interconnectors together

    Internal grid families with losses and bus-bar demand, next to two-way corridors with distance-based losses, in the same energy balance.

  4. One framework

    Roots, the relaxation that orders their costs, derived scenarios built from edits, and the base-year lock and pin, all as inputs that can be audited.

  5. Two data routes

    A custom South Asia dataset built on OSeMOSYS Global, and a route from regional statistics for Latin America and the Caribbean, built from OLADE data.

  6. Replication

    Inputs, settings and code in a public repository, with checks that rebuild the model inputs byte for byte and a site whose every value names its file.

  7. Limitations

    Transfers rather than power flow, time-slice resolution, one reserve pool and rule-based build caps, and what each means for the results.

  8. Conclusion

    What a shared framework makes possible for other regions of the Global South.
