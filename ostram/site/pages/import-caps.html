Import caps

OSTRAM can limit how much of a country's electricity comes from abroad, in two ways: as a share of a country's annual demand, counted over all its imports (the gross cap), or as a limit on single links. Both are scenario settings; the base model has no import cap of either kind.

Two kinds of cap

Gross

A share of demand

All power a country receives in a year, after the link losses, stays below a fraction of its annual demand. Transit counts as an import and an export; links between regions of the same country never count.

Per link

A limit on a corridor

The flow on one link in one direction stays below a limit. A setup policy can hold a link at what its existing capacity carries; a scenario edit can share an import limit across links in the pattern of a solved parent scenario.

Worked example: why the cap is divided by one minus the loss

Demand measured at the bus bar enters the model already reduced by the internal loss. A cap written as a share of that reduced demand would be tighter than intended. The model therefore receives the fraction divided by one minus the loss, so that the cap equals the fraction times the published demand.
