Inside a node

A node is a country, or a region of a large country. In OSTRAM, power inside a node does not go straight from a plant to a consumer: it is collected, crosses the node's own grid, and only then meets demand or leaves for a neighbour. OSTRAM models each of those steps as fuels and technologies, so the internal grid costs money, has a capacity and loses energy.

How to read the names

Every technology and fuel carries the node in its name. A technology code is a type prefix, a family and the node: PWRSPVBGDXX is a power plant (PWR) of the solar family (SPV) in Bangladesh (BGDXX). A country modelled as one node ends in XX; a country split into regions carries the region instead, as in INDEA. Electricity is a fuel with a tier number: ELCBGDXX00 to ELCBGDXX04 are the stages of the chain above. An interconnector names both ends: TRNBGDXXINDEA links Bangladesh and India East; mode 1 runs from the first to the second, mode 2 back.

What the chain means for results

  • Imports pay the receiving grid. A received import arrives on ELC..04 and goes to ELC..01, so it crosses the receiving node's grid like the node's own thermal output.
  • Exports leave from the demand fuel. They do not cross the sending node's grid a second time.
  • The grid has to be built. If plants and imports grow, the internal grid must grow with them, at the internal transmission cost. See internal grid families and losses and internal transmission costs.
  • Small hydro sits on the other side. The renewable side collects the fuels listed as renewable in the country configuration; small hydro is not among them, so it joins the other plants.
