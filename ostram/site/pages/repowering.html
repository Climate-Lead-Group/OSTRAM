Repowering and new lines

An internal grid can grow in two ways: by upgrading lines that already exist (repowering, for example with new conductors) or by building new corridors. OSTRAM gives each side of each node's grid a family for each, next to the family of existing lines.

How the families behave today

  • Same cost, life and loss. In South Asia the repowered and new-line families carry the same cost, a life of {{v:south_asia.internal.operational_life}} years and the same loss as the existing line. Only the existing line starts with capacity.
  • The split carries no meaning. Because the three families of a side are identical, the solver can move new capacity between them at no change in cost. Read and report their sum.
  • No build cap. Neither family has a build cap of its own. A scenario can switch them off with an edit, which is how the trade and transmission cap scenarios hold a node's grid at its existing size.

Design questions for a new application

An application with data on its grid can make the families mean more. The questions to settle are listed here, not answered.

  • Should repowering cost less than a new line, as reconductoring usually does?
  • Should new lines carry a lead time or a yearly build limit?
  • Should existing lines retire, so that repowering replaces something?
  • How should the base-year grid be sized when line data exist?
