Internal grid families and losses

Most power-system models treat a country as a copper plate: power made anywhere reaches demand anywhere at no cost. OSTRAM gives each node an internal grid with a capacity, a cost and a loss. The grid is split into families so that renewable output and other output can be tracked separately.

The families

Each side of the grid has an existing line, a repowered line and a new line. Within a side the three have the same cost, life and loss; only the existing line starts with capacity. Read the grid of a node as the sum of its side.

Where the loss applies

On the grid

The output ratio of every internal family is one minus the loss: what leaves the grid is {{v:south_asia.internal.efficiency}} of what enters it in South Asia.

On bus-bar demand

Published demand measured at the bus bar already includes grid losses. The rule writes it as its published value times one minus the loss, so the grid loss is not counted twice.

On exports from a bus-bar node

An interconnector sent from such a node draws one minus the loss per unit of activity, for the same reason.

How much grid gets built

The internal grid has no build rule and no fixed ratio to plants or demand. Its capacity must carry the busiest time slice, so it grows when the peak flow through the node grows, and each new gigawatt costs the internal transmission cost. What a rule does set is the grid of the base year: a margin of {{v:south_asia.internal.residual_peak_margin}} times the base-year peak, split between the renewable and the other side, computed in every run.

Worked example: one unit of power through a node

  1. 1

    A plant sends one unit

    It enters the grid on the renewable or the other side, depending on the plant.

  2. 2

    The grid delivers less

    {{v:south_asia.internal.efficiency}} of the unit reaches ELC..02; the rest is the loss of {{v:south_asia.internal.transmission_loss|pct}}.

  3. 3

    Dispatch passes it to demand

    DSPTRN mode 1 moves it to the demand fuel at no cost.

  4. 4

    Demand takes it, or a link sends it on

    A neighbour that receives it as an import puts it through its own grid and loses its own share again.
