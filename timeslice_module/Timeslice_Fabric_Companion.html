Phantom solar is the fraction of solar energy a flat-CF block credits to hours when the sun is down. The 3dp fabric credits 16 times more solar to dark hours than the adopted fabric, meaning a solver could dispatch PV capacity at 3 a.m. This is the main reason coarse fabrics are problematic.

The teaching point

Coarser fabrics do not dim the solar day. They invent solar at night. An 8-hour block that spans 00-08 includes 06-08 dawn, so it assigns a flat positive CF to five genuinely dark hours. A solver using 3dp/12ts could meet a 3 a.m. demand peak with PV capacity and call it feasible.

Finer fabrics isolate the dawn ramp from the solar core, eliminating phantom solar, but they scale the LP proportionally. The adopted 5dp/20ts is the deliberate compromise: a dedicated 06-17 solar block captures the full production curve without the solver cost of 24 timeslices.

The choice of timeslice fabric is not cosmetic. It changes what the model thinks is physically possible.
